How other apps handle locking. Password managers lock on a timer you choose, not every time their keyboard or fill panel hides. Your keyboard does the second today: hiding it ends the unlock, and switching apps hides it. A timer keeps the key only in the keyboard’s memory, never on disk, so a closed keyboard is a locked keyboard.
The limit on “intelligent” dates. iOS shows a keyboard only the field’s keyboard type, its content type, and the text already inside it. It does not show the page’s label or placeholder, such as “DD-MMM-YYYY” printed next to the field. When a form gives no signal, the keyboard falls back to your region, and holding the key lets you pick.

The fifth item · English name flagged as Chinese

I could not tell which screen you mean, and the code has two candidates. (a) The document’s Language row or Library badge says 中文 or Mixed. The app decides this from the share of Chinese characters in the scan. It counts Chinese punctuation, and Vision sometimes reads the passport’s < symbols as Chinese brackets, so a passport can tip over. (b) A field shows a Chinese label such as 姓名 under the English one. That is design rule 6, not a language flag. Please drop a screenshot into Unresolved Issues Screenshots, and I will fix it with the rule you gave: ID documents are English unless there are real Chinese characters.

Questions

Q1 · Keyboard lock window: Never, 1, 5 or 15 minutes after the last key. Recommended: 5 minutes. (“Never” means it locks only when the phone locks or iOS closes it.)
Q2 · Build the scan button in the keyboard? Recommended: yes. It stops the app switch that caused the lock problem in the first place.
Q3 · Month in capitals, as on the passport (12/MAR/1990), or title case (12/Mar/1990)? Recommended: capitals.
Q4 · Should DD/MMM/YYYY also become the keyboard’s fallback when a form gives no signal? Recommended: no. With no signal, a numeric date is the safer guess; keep DD/MM/YYYY as the fallback and offer MMM on hold.
